Players take turns activating one unit at a time, as described in [The Turn](../turn/). When you activate a unit it can:

- **move**, using up to its full Move, and
- use **one ability**: a ranged attack, a spell or another activated ability. It can do this before, after or in the middle of its move.

If the unit has a commander, the commander gets **one ability of its own** in the same activation.

A unit in combat can't move, and can only use the few abilities that work in combat. A fleeing unit can only try to [rally](#rallying-fleeing-units).

## Movement

A unit moves in two ways, which you can chain together in any order until its Move is used up:

<dl class="rule-grid">
  <dt>Advance</dt><dd>Move straight forward. Each inch costs 1" of Move, or 2" while in a forest. Units can't move backwards or sideways.</dd>
  <dt>Pivot</dt><dd>Turn on the spot around the unit's centre, by any angle. A pivot costs a quarter of the unit's Move for every 90°.</dd>
</dl>

<PivotDiagram />

- An advance stops at the first unit or impassable terrain in the way. You can't push through friends.
- A pivot is only blocked by **enemy** units. You can swing past friends and terrain.
- A unit can't end its move overlapping another unit, or within about half an inch of one.
- **War Machines** have Move 0 but pivot for free, so they can always turn to face a target.
- A unit that pursued a fleeing enemy in this turn's Charge phase can't move, but it can still use abilities.

In the game you drag the move handle to advance, and drag the pivot handles to turn. Right-click undoes the last step; double-click confirms.

<Callout type="example">
A unit of Cavalry (Move 16") pivots 45° to face a gap, costing 2". It advances 10" through it, then pivots another 90° to face the enemy flank for 4". Total: 16".
</Callout>

## Ranged attacks

A unit with a ranged weapon can shoot it as its ability for the activation. The target must be:

- within the weapon's **range**, measured to the nearest point of the target;
- in the shooter's **line of sight**: the same 90° arc from the front edge used for charges, blocked by units, forests, buildings and cliffs. See [Who it can reach](../charge-phase/#who-it-can-reach);
- **not in combat**. A unit in combat can't shoot, and you can't shoot into a melee.

A unit may still move after shooting if it has Move left.

<RangeDiagram />

### Number of shots

Each model in the unit's **first two ranks** shoots once. Models further back can't see past their comrades. Some weapons fire two or more shots per model. A commander shoots with its own weapon, using its own ability.

### The roll to hit

Roll a die for each shot. The number needed depends on the shooter's **Skill**:

Then apply modifiers. Each one makes the roll 1 harder or easier, but a roll is never worse than 6+ or better than 2+:

<dl class="rule-grid">
  <dt>Long range −1</dt><dd>The target is more than half the weapon's range away. Only weapons with a range of 12" or more suffer this.</dd>
  <dt>Cover −1</dt><dd>The target is in a forest, or always counts as in cover.</dd>
</dl>

Each hit then makes a [damage save](../combat-phase/#damage-save) against the **weapon's Power**, exactly as in melee.

<Callout type="example">
Ten archers with Skill 4 shoot longbows at Orcs in a forest 20" away. A longbow has range 30", so 20" is long range. Skill 4 hits on 3+, then −1 for long range and −1 for cover: they need **5+**.

<div class="dice-row"><Dice values={[5, 2, 6, 3, 1, 5, 4, 2, 6, 3]} hit={5} /> <span>Four hits. The Orcs now make four damage saves against Power 3.</span></div>
</Callout>

### War machines and fire

Cannons, catapults, mortars, flamers and other blast weapons don't roll to hit. Roll the dice shown on the weapon, such as **D3**, **2D3** or **D6**, and the total is the number of hits. These weapons ignore long range. Against a target in cover, each die rolled loses 1 hit.

## Abilities and spells

Many units and most commanders have **activated abilities**: spells, war cries, battle drugs and stranger things. Using one takes the unit's ability for the activation, just like shooting.

- **No casting roll.** An ability always works. Its limit is how many times it can be used: most can be used **once or twice per battle**, and the game shows the uses left.
- **Targets** depend on the ability. It might affect an enemy in line of sight, a friend within range, everything within range, or just the user.
- **Duration**: most effects last until the start of the next Strategic phase. Some last the whole battle, or until the target's next combat.
- **In combat**: units in combat can only use abilities marked as usable in combat, and most abilities can't target units in combat.

**Spellcasters** choose their spells in the Army Builder: one **cantrip** and one **major spell**, from their faction's list.

<Callout type="tip">
A unit with a commander can act twice in one activation. Cast the commander's spell, *then* shoot with the unit, *then* move. All in one turn of the alternating activations.
</Callout>

## Rallying fleeing units

A **fleeing** unit has lost its nerve, after failing a break test or fleeing from a charge. When you activate it, the only thing it can do is try to **rally**: take a [Discipline test](../units/#discipline-tests) with no penalty. Its Discipline plus its ranks is the target.

- **Pass**: the unit rallies and fights on as normal from now on.
- **Fail**: it keeps fleeing.

Either way, its activation ends and it can't move this turn.

### Flight

At the **end of the Strategic phase**, every unit still fleeing runs its full Move toward the nearest table edge, unless it already fled this turn. A fleeing unit runs around impassable terrain and ignores difficult terrain. If it reaches the edge of the battlefield, it is **gone**: it counts as destroyed.

A unit still fleeing at the end of the battle counts as lost, so rallying it is worth points.
